The mathematics of change

How fast, right now?

A derivative is the slope of a curve at a single point, the rate of change at an instant. Zoom in until a curve turns straight, watch slopes become a new function, and see why the rules work.

1

Zoom until it’s straight

Pick a point on a smooth curve and zoom in. The bend fades away, and the curve becomes almost exactly a straight line, its tangent. The slope of that line is the derivative f ′(a).

tangent:  y = f(a) + f ′(a)·(x − a)“Differentiable” means: zoom in far enough and the curve can’t be told apart from this line.

Not every curve does this. A corner, like |x| at 0, looks the same at every zoom. Its left and right slopes disagree, so there is no derivative there.

The idea

A derivative is the slope a curve settles into when you look closely enough. Smooth curves are locally linear. This is why physics can treat any small change as if it were a straight-line change.

Try it

Choose |x| (corner), set a = 0 and zoom in. Does it ever straighten?

No. Every zoom shows the same V shape. The left slope is −1 and the right slope is +1. They disagree, so |x| has no derivative at 0.

For sin x at a = 0, what is the slope?

cos 0 = 1. Near 0 the sine curve is the line y = x, which is exactly the small-angle rule sin θ ≈ θ.

2

The derivative is a function

Slide the point along the curve and record the tangent’s slope each time. Those slopes trace out a new graph: the derivative f ′(x).

Read the two together. Where f rises, f ′ is above zero. Where it falls, f ′ is below zero. At a peak or a valley the tangent is flat, so f ′ = 0.

maximum or minimum  ⟹  f ′(x) = 0The reverse isn’t always true: x³ has a flat tangent at 0 but no peak or valley there.
f risingf fallingtangent / f ′(x)Drag on the top graph

The idea

Differentiating turns one function into another. The new one tells you, at every x, how steeply the old one is changing. Its zeros are where the old one can have peaks and valleys. This is how you find the highest point a projectile reaches.

Try it

For x³ − 3x, where is the tangent flat?

At x = ±1, where 3x² − 3 = 0. There is a maximum at x = −1 (value 2) and a minimum at x = 1 (value −2).

Where does sin x rise fastest?

Where its derivative cos x is largest, at x = 0, ±2π, …, where the slope is 1.

3

Rules from pictures

Grow a square of side x by a tiny dx. The area gains two thin strips of size x·dx, plus a tiny corner dx².

d(x²) = 2x·dx + dx²  ⟹  d(x²)/dx = 2x + dx → 2xAs dx shrinks, the corner shrinks much faster than the strips, so in the limit only 2x remains.

The idea

Every derivative rule is a statement about which pieces of a small change survive as dx → 0. Pieces with one factor of dx survive. Pieces with dx² or smaller vanish. The product rule (fg)′ = f ′g + fg ′ is just the two strips of a growing rectangle.

Try it

Set x = 1 and shrink dx to 0.01. What share of the change is the corner?

About 0.5%: dx² / (2x·dx + dx²) = 0.0001 / 0.0201. By dx = 0.001 it is 0.05%.

In the product picture, what is d(fg) made of?

g·df + f·dg + df·dg. Dividing by dx and letting dx → 0 leaves f ′g + fg ′.

4

Motion and orbits

Velocity is the derivative of position, and acceleration is the derivative of velocity. On the graphs, each is the slope of the one above it.

v = dx/dt,    a = dv/dtThe yellow tangent on each graph has the slope that the graph below it is plotting.

For a planet seen edge-on, its distance along our line of sight goes up and down like a sine wave. The derivative is the radial velocity that astronomers measure from Doppler shifts. This is how most exoplanets were first found.

The idea

Derivatives are how physics describes change. A thrown ball has constant acceleration, so its velocity is a straight line and its height a parabola. A planet’s line-of-sight motion is a sine wave, and its derivative, the radial velocity, is a cosine wave shifted by a quarter period.

Try it

When is the ball’s velocity zero, and how high is it then?

At t = 20/9.8 ≈ 2.04 s, at the top, h ≈ 20.4 m. The acceleration is still −9.8 m/s² there: gravity never switches off.

For the planet, when is the radial velocity largest?

When it crosses z = 0 and is moving straight toward or away from us. For Earth’s orbit that is 2π AU/yr ≈ 29.8 km/s.

5

Linear approximation

Near a, the tangent line is an excellent stand-in for the curve. That gives a quick estimate for f a short step h away:

f(a + h) ≈ f(a) + f ′(a)·hThe error shrinks like h²: halve the step and the error drops about four times.

Astronomers use this constantly. sin θ ≈ θ and tan θ ≈ θ for tiny angles give parallax distances d ≈ 1/p and angular sizes θ ≈ D/d.

The idea

The derivative is the best linear prediction of how a function changes. Its error is second order, about ½f ″(a)h², so for small steps it is extraordinarily good.

Try it

Use sin θ ≈ θ at θ = 10° (0.1745 rad). How big is the error?

sin(0.1745) = 0.1736, so the error is 0.0009, about 0.5%. At 1° it is only 0.005%.

Estimate √4.1 with the tangent at 4.

2 + 0.1/4 = 2.025. The true value is 2.02485, so the error is only 0.00015.
